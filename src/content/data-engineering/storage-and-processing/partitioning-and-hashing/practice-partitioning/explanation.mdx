The problems use the layout of a partitioned table: one folder per combination of values of the <Term id="partition-column">partition columns</Term>, such as `day=…/country=…/`, and files of at most a <Term id="target-file-size">target size</Term> $T$ inside each folder.

## Counting

Columns with $d_1, \dots, d_k$ distinct values make at most $P = d_1 \cdots d_k$ partitions. A
table of $B$ bytes spread evenly gives partitions of $S = B / P$ bytes, written as

$$
\left\lceil \frac{S}{T} \right\rceil \text{ files each, and at least one.}
$$

## Pruning

A filter <Term id="partition-pruning">prunes</Term> when it can rule out a folder from its name alone: a condition on a partition
column, at any level of the path, joined to the rest of the filter with AND. A condition on any
other column, or one joined with OR to such a condition, reads every partition.

After pruning, the data read is the number of partitions kept times their size.

## Choosing a layout

Keep $S \geq T$, that is $P \leq B / T$. Among the layouts that fit, the finest prunes the most.
Columns that do not fit the budget are better used to sort the files, so that footer statistics
skip row groups inside each partition.
