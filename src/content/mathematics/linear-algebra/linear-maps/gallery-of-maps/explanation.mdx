A few <Term id="linear-transformation">linear maps</Term> come up again and again: rotations,
reflections, shears, scalings and projections. Each one is fixed by where it sends
$\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$, so its <Term id="matrix">matrix</Term> can be read from the
picture. This item builds the five matrices and asks of each what it keeps: lengths, angles,
areas, orientation.

## Rotation

A <StepLink step="rotation">rotation</StepLink> by $\theta$ counterclockwise moves
$\hat{\imath}$ along the <Term id="unit-circle">unit circle</Term> to $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, and $\hat{\jmath}$, a quarter
turn ahead of it, to $(-\sin\theta, \cos\theta)$:

$$
R_\theta = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Rotating a photo is this map applied to the position of every pixel. A rotation keeps lengths,
angles, areas and orientation: the letter turns and nothing about it changes.

## Reflection

The <StepLink step="reflection">reflection</StepLink> across the line through the origin at
angle $\varphi$ keeps that line and flips each point to the other side, at the same distance. A
mirror does this. Where $\hat{\imath}$ lands follows from the picture: the line is at angle
$\varphi$, so the image of $\hat{\imath}$ (angle $0$) is at angle $2\varphi$. So

$$
F_\varphi = \begin{bmatrix} \cos 2\varphi & \sin 2\varphi \\ \sin 2\varphi & -\cos 2\varphi \end{bmatrix}.
$$

With $\varphi = 45^\circ$ (the line $y = x$) it swaps the coordinates. Lengths, angles and
areas stay, but the order of $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$ is reversed: turning from
$A\hat{\imath}$ to $A\hat{\jmath}$ is now clockwise, and the letter reads as in a mirror. That
is a flip of orientation.

## Shear

A <StepLink step="shear">shear</StepLink> keeps $\hat{\imath}$ and tilts $\hat{\jmath}$:

$$
S_k = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad (x, y) \mapsto (x + ky,\ y).
$$

Each horizontal line slides by $k$ times its height. The unit square becomes a parallelogram
with the same base and the same height, so its area stays $1$, and so does the area of every
shape (cut it into thin horizontal strips; each strip only slides). Lengths and angles change.

## Scaling

A <StepLink step="scaling">scaling</StepLink> stretches each axis on its own:

$$
D = \begin{bmatrix} s_x & 0 \\ 0 & s_y \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The unit square becomes an $s_x$ by $s_y$ rectangle, so areas are multiplied by $s_xs_y$. With
$s_x = s_y$ every length is multiplied by the same factor, so shapes and angles stay; otherwise a
circle becomes an ellipse and angles change.

## Projection onto a line

The <StepLink step="projection">projection</StepLink> onto the line along $\mathbf{u}$ sends
$\mathbf{x}$ to the foot of the perpendicular from $\mathbf{x}$ to the line. Vectors gave its
length: the <Term id="scalar-projection">scalar projection</Term> $\frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{x}}{\lVert \mathbf{u} \rVert}$.
So

$$
P\mathbf{x} = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{u}}\,\mathbf{u},
\qquad
P = \frac{1}{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{u}} \begin{bmatrix} u_1^2 & u_1u_2 \\ u_1u_2 & u_2^2 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The columns are $P\hat{\imath}$ and $P\hat{\jmath}$, from the formula with $\mathbf{x} = \hat{\imath}$
and $\mathbf{x} = \hat{\jmath}$. With the sun straight overhead, a shadow on flat ground is the
projection onto the ground: $\mathbf{u} = (1, 0)$ and $P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

A projection sends the whole perpendicular direction to $\mathbf{0}$, so the plane lands on a
line. Areas become $0$, and the map cannot be undone: many points share each shadow. Projecting
twice changes nothing more, $P(P\mathbf{x}) = P\mathbf{x}$, since a point already on the line
stays. A shadow cast by a low sun, along a slanted direction, is also a linear map onto the
ground (an oblique projection); "projection" here always means the perpendicular one.

The projection also gives the reflection across the same line: $\mathbf{x}$ and its mirror image
have $P\mathbf{x}$ as their midpoint, so $F\mathbf{x} = 2P\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}$, that is
$F = 2P - I$ (with $I$ the identity).

## What each map keeps

| Map        | Lengths | Angles        | Areas          | Orientation |
| ---------- | ------- | ------------- | -------------- | ----------- |
| Rotation   | kept    | kept          | kept           | kept        |
| Reflection | kept    | kept          | kept           | flipped     |
| Shear      | changed | changed       | kept           | kept        |
| Scaling    | changed | kept if equal | times $s_xs_y$ | kept        |
| Projection | changed | changed       | $0$            | lost        |

All of this can be read from the images of the basis. Lengths are kept exactly when
$A\hat{\imath}$ and $A\hat{\jmath}$ have length $1$ and meet at a right angle; angles when they
have equal lengths and meet at a right angle. The area factor is the area of the parallelogram
they span, and the orientation is kept when the turn from $A\hat{\imath}$ to $A\hat{\jmath}$ is
still counterclockwise. The <StepLink step="keeps">last step</StepLink> reads these off live.
Determinants, the next unit, measures area and orientation together with one number.
