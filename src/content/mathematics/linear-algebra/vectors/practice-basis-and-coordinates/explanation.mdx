Problems on <Term id="basis">bases</Term> and <Term id="coordinates">coordinates</Term> in the
plane and in space.

## Answers

- Type coordinates and vectors as their components in order: $(2, -1, 3)$.
- Questions about whether vectors form a basis are multiple choice; the worked solution shows the
  check.

## Facts

If $\mathcal{B} = (\mathbf{b}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{b}_n)$ is a basis, the coordinates
$[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} = (c_1, \ldots, c_n)$ are the weights with

$$
c_1\mathbf{b}_1 + \cdots + c_n\mathbf{b}_n = \mathbf{x}.
$$

Going from coordinates to components is a combination; going from components to coordinates
means solving one equation per component. A basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ has exactly $n$ vectors,
and they must be linearly <Term id="linear-independence">independent</Term>: two vectors of the
plane when neither is a multiple of the other, three vectors of space when none is a combination
of the other two.
