Problems on the <Term id="dot-product">dot product</Term>, <Term id="span">span</Term> and
independence, and lines and planes in space.

## Answers

- Angles are in degrees: type $60$, not $60^\circ$ or $\frac{\pi}{3}$.
- Type a vector or a point as its components in order: $(3, -1, 2)$. Fractions work too.
- Questions about span and independence are multiple choice; the worked solution shows the
  check.

## Formulas

$$
\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = v_1 w_1 + \cdots + v_n w_n = \lVert \mathbf{v} \rVert \, \lVert \mathbf{w} \rVert \cos\theta,
$$

$$
\text{projection of } \mathbf{w} \text{ on } \mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{v}}\,\mathbf{v}.
$$

Vectors are <Term id="orthogonal">orthogonal</Term> when their dot product is $0$. A vector is in
the span of $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$ when $a\mathbf{v} + b\mathbf{w}$ equals it for some
weights: solve two components and check the third. Three vectors are
linearly <Term id="linear-dependence">dependent</Term> exactly when one is a combination of the
others.

The plane through $\mathbf{p}$ with <Term id="normal-vector">normal</Term> $\mathbf{n}$ is
$n_1 x + n_2 y + n_3 z = \mathbf{n} \cdot \mathbf{p}$. A
parametric <Term id="parametric-line">line</Term> $\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{v}$ meets it where the
components of $\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{v}$ satisfy that equation.
