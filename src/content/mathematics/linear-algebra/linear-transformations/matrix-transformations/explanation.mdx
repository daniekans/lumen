A $2 \times 2$ matrix is a compact description of a function that moves every point of the plane.
The functions it can describe are the linear ones: they fix the origin and keep grid lines straight,
parallel and evenly spaced.

## Columns record the basis

Write any vector as $\mathbf{v} = x\hat{\imath} + y\hat{\jmath}$. Linearity means

$$
A\mathbf{v} = x\,A\hat{\imath} + y\,A\hat{\jmath},
$$

so $A$ is fully determined by two vectors: where <C c="green">$\hat{\imath}$</C> lands and where

<C c="red">$\hat{\jmath}$</C> lands. Those are the columns of the matrix:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
A\hat{\imath} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix}, \quad
A\hat{\jmath} = \begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Drag the tips of the basis vectors to edit the columns directly. The dashed path to $A\mathbf{v}$
shows the recipe $2\,A\hat{\imath} + A\hat{\jmath}$ for $\mathbf{v} = (2, 1)$.

## Determinant

The unit square spanned by $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$ maps to the parallelogram spanned by the
two columns. Its signed area is

$$
\det A = ad - bc.
$$

Because the grid stays evenly spaced, every region scales by the same factor $|\det A|$.

- $\det A > 0$: orientation is kept. Turning from $\hat{\imath}$ to $\hat{\jmath}$ is still
  counterclockwise.
- $\det A < 0$: the plane is <StepLink step="sign">flipped</StepLink>, like a reflection.
- $\det A = 0$: the plane collapses onto a line or a point. Different inputs share the same output,
  so $A$ has no inverse.

## Presets

| Preset  | Matrix                                                 | $\det$ |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| Rotate  | $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$        | $1$    |
| Shear   | $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$         | $1$    |
| Scale   | $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1.5 \end{bmatrix}$       | $3$    |
| Reflect | $\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$        | $-1$   |
| Project | $\begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & 0.4 \\ 0.4 & 0.2 \end{bmatrix}$ | $0$    |

The shear and the rotation change shapes but not areas, which is why both have determinant $1$.
