Problems on the <Term id="determinant">determinant</Term>: computing it, the rules it obeys, and
areas and orientation in the plane.

## Answers

- Every answer is a number or a choice. Exact fractions such as $\frac{5}{2}$ can be typed as they
  are.
- Areas are positive; the sign of a determinant only says whether a map flips orientation.

## Formulas

$$
\det \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc,
$$

$$
\det A = \sum_{j} a_{ij}C_{ij}, \qquad C_{ij} = (-1)^{i + j}\det M_{ij}.
$$

A <Term id="cofactor">cofactor</Term> expansion works along any row or column.
A <Term id="triangular-matrix">triangular matrix</Term> has the product of its diagonal as its
determinant, and elimination gives $\det A = (-1)^{\text{swaps}}$ times the product of the pivots.

| Operation                              | Effect on $\det$ |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Swap two rows (or columns)             | times $-1$       |
| Multiply one row by $c$                | times $c$        |
| Add a multiple of one row to another   | none             |
| Multiply an $n \times n$ matrix by $c$ | times $c^n$      |
| Transpose                              | none             |

In the plane, $\det[\mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p}\ \ \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{p}]$ is positive when
$\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{r}$ turn counterclockwise, negative when they turn clockwise and $0$
when they are on one line. A polygon with corners $(x_k, y_k)$ in order has area
$\frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_k (x_ky_{k+1} - x_{k+1}y_k)\right|$ (the shoelace formula), and a linear map
multiplies every area by $|\det A|$.
