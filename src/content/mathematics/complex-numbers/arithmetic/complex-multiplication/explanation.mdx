Adding complex numbers adds their arrows tip to tail. Multiplication is less obvious from the
algebra, but it has a clean geometric meaning: it combines a rotation and a scaling.

## The algebra

With $z = a + bi$, $w = c + di$ and $i^2 = -1$,

$$
zw = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)\,i.
$$

For the starting values $z = 2 + i$ and $w = 1 + i$ this gives $zw = 1 + 3i$.

## Polar form

Write each number by its length and angle: $z = r\,(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$ with $r = |z|$ and
$\theta = \arg z$. Expanding the product of two numbers in this form and applying the angle
addition formulas gives

$$
|zw| = |z|\,|w|, \qquad \arg(zw) = \arg z + \arg w.
$$

So multiplying <C c="blue">$z$</C> by <C c="yellow">$w$</C> turns $z$ through the angle $\arg w$
and stretches it by $|w|$. The readouts show both sides of each identity. Angles are shown in
$[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$, so the sum of arguments wraps around after a full turn.

## Every point at once

The rule does not depend on $z$. Multiplication by $w$ is a map of the whole plane that rotates by
$\arg w$ and scales by $|w|$. Because it rotates and scales uniformly, it preserves angles and
shapes: the unit square becomes a square with one corner at $0$ and another at $w$, since
$1 \cdot w = w$.

The <StepLink step="plane">last step</StepLink> animates the map through the powers $w^t$ for
$t$ from $0$ to $1$, which follow a spiral from $1$ to $w$.

## Special cases

- $|w| = 1$: a pure rotation. Multiplying by $i$ turns the plane a quarter turn counterclockwise.
- $w$ real and positive: a pure scaling.
- $w = -1$: a half turn, which is why $(-1)(-1) = 1$ is the same as two half turns.
