An inverse function answers the question in reverse: given an output, which input produced it?
When that question always has exactly one answer, the answers form a new function, $f^{-1}$.

## Definition

$f^{-1}$ is the inverse of $f$ when each one undoes the other:

$$
f^{-1}(f(x)) = x \quad\text{and}\quad f(f^{-1}(y)) = y.
$$

The notation $f^{-1}$ does not mean $\frac{1}{f}$. For $f(x) = 2x + 1$,

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 1}{2}, \qquad f(1) = 3, \quad f^{-1}(3) = 1.
$$

## Finding a formula

Write $y = f(x)$ and solve for $x$:

$$
y = 2x + 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{y - 1}{2}.
$$

Then rename the variable to get $f^{-1}(x) = \frac{x - 1}{2}$. Check the result by <Term id="composition">composing</Term>:
$f(f^{-1}(x)) = 2 \cdot \frac{x - 1}{2} + 1 = x$.

## The graph is a reflection

If $(a, b)$ is on the graph of $f$, then $f(a) = b$, so $f^{-1}(b) = a$ and $(b, a)$ is on the
graph of $f^{-1}$. Swapping the two coordinates reflects a point across the dashed line $y = x$, so the graph of $f^{-1}$ in <C c="teal">teal</C> is the mirror image of
the graph of $f$ in <C c="blue">blue</C>.

## When an inverse exists

$f$ has an inverse exactly when it is one-to-one: different inputs give different outputs. On the
graph, every horizontal line meets it at most once. $f(x) = x^2 - 1$ fails, because $f(2) = f(-2)$.

Restricting the domain can fix this. On $x \geq 0$ the outputs no longer repeat, and solving
$y = x^2 - 1$ with $x \geq 0$ gives

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x + 1}, \qquad x \geq -1.
$$

The <Term id="domain">domain</Term> of $f^{-1}$ is the <Term id="range">range</Term> of $f$, and the range of $f^{-1}$ is the domain of $f$.
