A rational expression is a fraction whose numerator and denominator are polynomials, such as
$\frac{x + 2}{x^2 + x - 2}$. It follows the rules of numeric fractions, with one addition: it is
undefined wherever the denominator is zero, and simplifying must not lose track of those values.

## Simplifying

Factor both parts and cancel common factors:

$$
\frac{x^2 - x - 6}{x - 3} = \frac{(x - 3)(x + 2)}{x - 3} = x + 2, \qquad x \neq 3.
$$

Only factors cancel, never terms: in $\frac{x + 2}{x}$ the $x$ is part of a sum, and
$\frac{x + 2}{x} \neq 2$.

## Excluded values

The excluded values come from the original denominator, before cancelling. On the graph:

- a factor that cancels leaves a <C c="blue">hole</C>: the curve continues on both sides, with one
  point missing;
- a factor that stays in the denominator gives a vertical asymptote, where the values grow without
  bound.

In <StepLink step="excluded">the second step</StepLink>, $\frac{x + 2}{(x + 2)(x - 1)}$ has a hole at
$x = -2$ and an asymptote at $x = 1$.

## Multiplying and dividing

$$
\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}, \qquad \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} =
\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}.
$$

Factor everything first, then cancel across the product. Every denominator of the original
expression contributes excluded values, and so does the numerator of a divisor $\frac{c}{d}$,
because dividing by zero is not allowed.

## Adding and subtracting

Rewrite both fractions over a common denominator, the product of the distinct factors:

$$
\frac{1}{x - 1} + \frac{1}{x + 1} = \frac{x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} + \frac{x - 1}{(x - 1)(x + 1)}
= \frac{2x}{x^2 - 1}.
$$

The graph in <StepLink step="add">the last step</StepLink> shows why there is no shortcut: at every
$x$ the <C c="blue">sum</C> is the <C c="teal">first height</C> plus
the <C c="yellow">second height</C>, and only the common-denominator form has those values.
