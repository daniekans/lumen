Two skills: using the <Term id="exponent">exponent laws</Term>, and writing radicals in simplest form.

## Exponent laws

$$
a^m a^n = a^{m + n}, \qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m - n},
$$

$$
(a^m)^n = a^{mn}, \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n.
$$

With $a \neq 0$, $a^0 = 1$ and $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$. A <Term id="negative-exponent">negative exponent</Term> on a fraction flips it:
$\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}$.

## Rational exponents

$$
a^{p/q} = \left(\sqrt[q]{a}\right)^p.
$$

Take the <Term id="nth-root">root</Term> first: $27^{2/3} = 3^2 = 9$.

## Simplest radical form

A radical is in <Term id="simplest-radical-form">simplest form</Term> when no square factor is left under the root and no root is left in a
denominator.

- Take out the largest square factor: $\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2} = 6\sqrt{2}$.
- Multiply under one root, then simplify: $\sqrt{6}\,\sqrt{15} = \sqrt{90} = 3\sqrt{10}$.
- Rationalize: $\frac{4}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{4\sqrt{6}}{6} = \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}$.
- Only like radicals add: $\sqrt{12} + \sqrt{27} = 2\sqrt{3} + 3\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3}$.

## Answering

Number answers accept fractions and decimals. Some choices list forms that are equal but
not simplest; pick the simplest one.
