How do you multiply, divide or raise powers without writing out every factor? Each exponent law
follows from one definition, and each one is a statement about counting factors.

## Definition

For a whole number $m$,

$$
a^m = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdots a}_{m \text{ factors}}.
$$

The base is $a$ and the exponent $m$ counts the factors, drawn as tiles.

## Product rule

$$
a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m + n}
$$

Writing <C c="blue">$m$ factors</C> next to <C c="yellow">$n$ more</C> gives $m + n$ factors. The
rule needs the same base: $2^3 \cdot 3^2$ does not combine.

## Quotient rule

$$
\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m - n}, \qquad a \neq 0
$$

Each factor below the bar cancels one above it. In <StepLink step="quotient">the quotient
step</StepLink>, $\frac{a^5}{a^2}$ leaves three factors.

## Zero and negative exponents

The quotient rule only works for every $m$ and $n$ if

$$
a^0 = 1, \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}, \qquad a \neq 0.
$$

When all factors cancel the quotient is $1$, and leftovers below the bar stay below. A negative
exponent means a reciprocal, never a negative number: $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$.

## Power rules

$$
(a^m)^n = a^{mn}, \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n, \qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}.
$$

$(a^m)^n$ is a grid of $n$ rows with $m$ factors each. The other two rules hold because the factors
of a product can be regrouped in any order.

## Common mistakes

- $a^m + a^n$ is not $a^{m + n}$: the laws are about products, not sums. $2^2 + 2^3 = 12$, while
  $2^5 = 32$.
- $(a + b)^2$ is not $a^2 + b^2$: the power of a product rule does not apply to sums.
- $-2^4 = -16$ but $(-2)^4 = 16$: the exponent applies only to what it touches.
