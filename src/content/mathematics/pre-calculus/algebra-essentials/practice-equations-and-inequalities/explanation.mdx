Three skills: solving equations, solving inequalities, and <Term id="absolute-value">absolute value</Term>.

## Equations

- Linear: collect the $x$ terms on one side and the constants on the other.
- Quadratic: <Term id="factoring">factor</Term> when the roots are integers; otherwise use
  $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
- Rational: multiply by the denominator, solve, then drop any candidate that makes a denominator
  zero.
- Radical: square both sides, solve, then check each candidate in the original equation. A square
  root is never negative.

## Inequalities

- Dividing or multiplying by a negative number reverses the inequality.
- Factor, mark the zeros, and test one value in each interval (a <Term id="sign-chart">sign chart</Term>).
- The zeros of a denominator are never in the solution set.
- $<$ and $>$ leave the endpoints out, written with parentheses; $\leq$ and $\geq$ include them,
  written with <Term id="interval-notation">square brackets</Term>.

## Absolute value

- $\lvert x - a \rvert$ is the distance from $x$ to $a$.
- $\lvert u \rvert = r$ means $u = r$ or $u = -r$.
- $\lvert x - a \rvert < r$ is the interval $(a - r, a + r)$; $\lvert x - a \rvert > r$ is the
  outside, $(-\infty, a - r) \cup (a + r, \infty)$.

## Answering

Lists of solutions take numbers separated by commas; fractions and roots such as
$\frac{-3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ are accepted. Solution sets of inequalities are asked as choices.
