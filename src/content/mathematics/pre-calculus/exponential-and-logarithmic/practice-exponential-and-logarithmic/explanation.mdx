Four skills: exponential models, the <Term id="euler-number">number $e$</Term>, logarithms, and
equations with an unknown exponent.

## Logarithms

- $\log_b x = y$ means $b^y = x$. Write both numbers as powers of one base when you can:
  $\log_8 4 = \frac{2}{3}$ because $8 = 2^3$ and $4 = 2^2$.
- The <Term id="logarithm-laws">laws</Term>: products become sums, quotients differences, and
  powers come down as factors.
- <Term id="change-of-base">Change of base</Term>: $\log_b x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln b}$.

## Models

- $f(x) = a \cdot b^x$: $a = f(0)$, and $b$ is the factor per step of $1$. The ratio of two values
  gives $b$ raised to the gap between their inputs.
- A <Term id="half-life">half-life</Term> $h$ gives $A_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t / h}$.
- <Term id="continuous-compounding">Continuous growth</Term> is $Pe^{rt}$; it doubles after
$\frac{\ln 2}{r}$.

## Equations

- Isolate the power, then take a logarithm (or match powers of the same base).
- Rewrite $\log_b (\ldots) = k$ as $\ldots = b^k$, and check every root in the original equation: a
  root that makes an argument negative or zero is an
  <Term id="extraneous-solution">extraneous solution</Term>.

## Answering

Most answers are numbers, and exact forms are accepted: $\frac{\ln 5}{2}$, $\log_2 10$ or $\frac{2}{3}$.
When a problem asks for two decimal places, the value rounded to two places is accepted.
