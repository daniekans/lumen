A linear function adds the same amount over every step of $1$. An <Term id="exponential-function">exponential function</Term> multiplies by the same factor instead. Populations, compound interest,
radioactive decay and the $2^n$ values that $n$ bits can store all change this way.

## Equal steps, equal factors

$$
f(x) = a \cdot b^x, \qquad a \neq 0,\ b > 0,\ b \neq 1.
$$

The <Term id="exponent">exponent laws</Term> give the defining property:

$$
f(x + 1) = a \cdot b^{x + 1} = b \cdot f(x).
$$

So $b$ is the <Term id="growth-factor">growth factor</Term> per step of $1$, and $a = f(0)$ is the
starting value. Going left divides by $b$, which is why $b^{-1} = \frac{1}{b}$ and why the graph
approaches the $x$-axis without reaching it: the range of $b^x$ is $(0, \infty)$.

## Growth or decay

- $b > 1$: growth. A rate of $5\%$ per year is $b = 1.05$.
- $0 < b < 1$: decay. Losing $20\%$ per year is $b = 0.8$.

Since $\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)^x = b^{-x}$, a decay curve is a growth curve reflected across the
$y$-axis, as the dashed curve in <StepLink step="base">the second step</StepLink> shows.

## Doubling time and half-life

If $b^d = 2$, then for every $x$

$$
f(x + d) = a \cdot b^x \cdot b^d = 2 f(x).
$$

The <Term id="doubling-time">doubling time</Term> $d$ is the same from every starting point. For
decay the same reasoning with $b^d = \frac{1}{2}$ gives the <Term id="half-life">half-life</Term>.
A half-life $h$ gives the model

$$
A(t) = A_0 \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t / h},
$$

so after $3$ half-lives $\frac{1}{8}$ of $A_0$ is left. Finding $d$ from $b$ means solving
$b^d = 2$ for an exponent, which is what logarithms do.

## Faster than any power

Compare $2^x$ with $x^2$. Moving from $x$ to $x + 1$ multiplies $2^x$ by $2$, but multiplies
$x^2$ by $\left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2$, which tends to $1$. Once that factor drops below $2$
(for $x$ above about $2.41$) the exponential gains on the power at every step, and it passes it for good at
$x = 4$. The same happens for $x^{10}$ or any power, only later.
