Everything here follows from one formula. For a function $f$ and a point $x_0$,

$$
\frac{f(x_0 + h) - f(x_0)}{h}
$$

is the slope of the secant through $x_0$ and $x_0 + h$. Its limit as $h \to 0$ is $f'(x_0)$.

## Method

1. Write $f(x_0 + h)$ and expand it.
2. Subtract $f(x_0)$. Every term without $h$ cancels.
3. Divide by $h$. This is allowed because $h \neq 0$ inside the limit.
4. Let $h \to 0$.

## Useful expansions

$$
(x + h)^2 = x^2 + 2xh + h^2
$$

$$
(x + h)^3 = x^3 + 3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3
$$

## Answering

Type answers in the math field: `/` starts a fraction, `^` an exponent. Equivalent forms are
accepted, so $2x + 3$ and $3 + 2x$ both count. The keyboard button opens an on-screen keyboard.
