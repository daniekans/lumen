These problems add the derivatives of exponentials, logarithms and trigonometric functions, and
the <Term id="chain-rule">chain rule</Term> for compositions.

## Derivatives to know

$$
\frac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}b^x = (\ln b)\,b^x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x}
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x}
$$

Angles are in radians. For $x > 0$ the power rule $\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^r\right) = rx^{r-1}$ holds for
every real $r$, so $\sqrt[3]{x^2} = x^{\frac{2}{3}}$ has derivative $\frac{2}{3}x^{-\frac{1}{3}}$.

## The chain rule

$$
\frac{d}{dx} f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)
$$

1. Name the inner function $u = g(x)$, the part inside the parentheses, root or exponent.
2. Differentiate the outer function as if $u$ were the variable, and keep $u$ inside.
3. Multiply by $u'$.

For example, $\frac{d}{dx}\ln\left(x^2 + 1\right) = \frac{1}{x^2 + 1} \cdot 2x$. The most common
mistake is evaluating $f'$ at $x$ instead of at $g(x)$.

## Products of compositions

When a product contains a composition, use the product rule first, then the chain rule inside
it: $\frac{d}{dx}\left(x e^{3x}\right) = e^{3x} + x \cdot 3e^{3x}$.
