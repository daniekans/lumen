These problems use the method of the item
[Optimization](/mathematics/calculus-1/applications-of-derivatives/optimization). Most of the work
is the model; the derivative comes at the end.

## The method

1. Name the variables and write the <Term id="objective-function">objective</Term>.
2. Use the constraint to leave one variable.
3. Find its interval from the situation: lengths and prices cannot be negative, a cut cannot pass
   the middle of the sheet, a regulation can cap a price.
4. Find the <Term id="critical-point">critical points</Term> inside the interval and compare the
   objective there and at the ends. A critical point outside the interval does not count.
5. On an open interval, check that the objective grows toward both edges, or use the
   <Term id="first-derivative-test">first derivative test</Term>.

## Two shortcuts

- A distance $D$ and its square $D^2$ have their minimum at the same point, because squaring is
  increasing for $D \ge 0$. Minimize $D^2$: no square root to differentiate.
- The <Term id="least-squares">least squares</Term> constant for data $y_1, \dots, y_n$ is their
  mean, from $S'(c) = -2\sum (y_i - c) = 0$.
