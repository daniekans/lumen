These problems use the items
[L'Hôpital's rule](/mathematics/calculus-1/applications-of-derivatives/lhopital) and
[Newton's method](/mathematics/calculus-1/applications-of-derivatives/newtons-method).

## Limits

1. Substitute first. Use <Term id="lhopitals-rule">L'Hôpital's rule</Term> only on
   $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$, and check the form again before each further use.
2. $0 \cdot \infty$: move one factor to the denominator. $\infty - \infty$: combine into one
   fraction.
3. $1^\infty$, $0^0$, $\infty^0$: find the limit $L$ of the logarithm, then the answer is $e^L$.
   Type it as `e^{6}`, for example.
4. If the ratio of the derivatives has no limit, the rule says nothing. Try algebra: divide the
   top and the bottom by the fastest-growing term.

## Newton's method

- One step: $x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$. Keep fractions exact.
- For $\sqrt{a}$: $x_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(x_n + \frac{a}{x_n}\right)$.
- To four decimals: iterate until two iterates agree to five or six decimals.
- When no formula solves an equation (a loan's rate from its payment), clear the fractions first
  so $f'$ stays simple, and start on the side where the iterates move toward the root you want.
- To minimize $g$: apply the step to $g'$, so $x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{g'(x_n)}{g''(x_n)}$.
- A flat tangent stops the method; a start can also cycle, or move away from the root when $f'$
  does not exist there.
