Four skills: reading a <Term id="limit">limit</Term>, one-sided and infinite limits, limits at
infinity, and evaluating limits with algebra.

## The routine

1. Substitute. If the function is defined there (a polynomial, or a rational function with a
   nonzero denominator), the value is the limit: <Term id="direct-substitution">direct substitution</Term>.
2. A nonzero number over $0$ is an <Term id="infinite-limit">infinite limit</Term>. Find the sign of
   the bottom on each side; a square is positive on both.
3. $\frac{0}{0}$ is an <Term id="indeterminate-form">indeterminate form</Term>. Factor and cancel,
   multiply by the conjugate of a root, or combine fractions over a common denominator, then
   substitute again.

## One-sided limits

For a piecewise function, use only the piece on the side the limit comes from. The two-sided limit
exists exactly when both <Term id="one-sided-limit">one-sided limits</Term> agree, and the value at
the point itself never matters.

## Limits at infinity

For a <Term id="limit-at-infinity">limit at infinity</Term>, divide the top and the bottom by the
highest power of $x$ in the denominator. A lower degree on top gives $0$, equal degrees give the
ratio of the leading coefficients. Under a square root, $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, which is $-x$ when
$x \to -\infty$.

## Answering

Answers are numbers, and exact forms are accepted: $\frac{10}{7}$ or $-\frac{1}{16}$. Infinite
limits are multiple choice.
