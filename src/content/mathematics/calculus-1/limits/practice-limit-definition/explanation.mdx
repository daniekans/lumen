Two skills: the <Term id="epsilon-delta">epsilon-delta definition</Term> (with
its <Term id="epsilon-n">epsilon-N</Term> version at infinity), and comparing growth rates.

## Finding delta

For a given $\varepsilon$, the largest $\delta$ is the distance from $a$ to the nearest input where
$f(x)$ reaches $L \pm \varepsilon$.

- A line $mx + b$: $|f(x) - L| = |m|\,|x - a|$, so $\delta = \frac{\varepsilon}{|m|}$.
- A parabola $x^2$: solve $x^2 = a^2 \pm \varepsilon$ near $a$ and take the nearer solution. The
  side away from $0$ is steeper, so it is the nearer one.
- The proof rule for $x^2$: restrict $\delta \le 1$, so $|x + a| < 2|a| + 1 = C$, and take
  $\delta = \min\left(1, \frac{\varepsilon}{C}\right)$. It is not the largest $\delta$, only one
  that always works.

## Finding N

At infinity, simplify $|f(x) - L|$ to something like $\frac{b}{x}$ and solve
$\frac{b}{x} < \varepsilon$: every $x > \frac{b}{\varepsilon}$ works, so $N = \frac{b}{\varepsilon}$.

## Growth rates

At infinity, $\ln x \ll x^p \ll b^x$ for every $p > 0$ and $b > 1$. A ratio with the slower class
on top tends to $0$; with the faster class on top it tends to
$\infty$. <Term id="big-o">Big-O</Term> asks for a constant: $f(x) \le C x^2$ for $x \ge x_0$ means $C$ is
at least the largest value of $\frac{f(x)}{x^2}$ there.

## Proofs

Proofs are graded by you. Work backward in scratch work, then write the proof forward: given
$\varepsilon > 0$, name $\delta$, and show that $0 < |x - a| < \delta$ gives
$|f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$. Compare with the solution step by step.

## Answering

Answers are numbers; exact forms such as $\frac{1}{4}$ are accepted.
