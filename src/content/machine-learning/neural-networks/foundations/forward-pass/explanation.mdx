A neural network is a <Term id="function">function</Term>. This one takes three numbers $x_1, x_2, x_3$ and returns two numbers
$\hat y_1, \hat y_2$. Everything in between is arithmetic that repeats the same pattern.

## Activations

Each neuron holds an activation, a number between 0 and 1. The fill of each circle shows it: an
empty circle is 0 and a solid one is 1. The input layer's activations are simply the inputs.

## What one neuron computes

A neuron in layer $l$ looks at every activation in layer $l-1$. It multiplies each by a weight, adds
the results and a bias, and passes the total $z$ through the sigmoid function:

$$
z_j = \sum_i w_{ji}\, a^{(l-1)}_i + b_j, \qquad a^{(l)}_j = \sigma(z_j) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z_j}}.
$$

A <C c="blue">positive weight</C> means "a larger input pushes this neuron up". A <C c="red">negative weight</C> pushes it down. The bias shifts how easily the neuron activates. The
sigmoid maps any real $z$ into $(0, 1)$, so large positive sums give activations near 1 and large
negative sums give activations near 0.

## The whole layer at once

Writing the weights of a layer as a <Term id="matrix">matrix</Term> $W$ and the biases as a vector $\mathbf b$, all neurons of
the layer are computed together:

$$
\mathbf a^{(l)} = \sigma\big(W^{(l)} \mathbf a^{(l-1)} + \mathbf b^{(l)}\big).
$$

The forward pass applies this three times here, once per pair of adjacent layers. The network in
this example has $3 \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot 2 = 36$ weights and $4 + 4 + 2 = 10$ biases.

## Where the weights come from

The weights here are fixed random numbers, so the outputs do not mean anything yet. Choosing weights
that make the outputs useful is the job of training, covered in
[Backpropagation](/machine-learning/neural-networks/training/backpropagation).
